## What receiver mode is

In receiver mode, the TV sits on an idle screen showing an address and a 6-digit pair code. You do the browsing, searching, and typing on your phone or computer, then choose **Play on TV** and pick that TV. Playback starts there, with transport controls (play/pause, seek, volume) working from the sender.

## The dedicated TV build (kiosk)

The dedicated TV build - what you get from the Google Play listing for Android TV, or the sideloadable `Extreme-InfiniTV-receiver.apk` from [GitHub Releases](https://github.com/infinitel8p/Extreme-InfiniTV/releases/latest) - boots straight into the pairing screen and doesn't include the browse UI at all. There's no exit button, and the back key doesn't leave the receiver screen. That's intentional: this build only does one job.

Building from source? The kiosk variant is a normal Android build with two extra environment variables:

```bash
XTREAM_TV_BUILD=true PUBLIC_APP_MODE=receiver pnpm tauri android build --apk
```

`PUBLIC_APP_MODE=receiver` bakes the kiosk frontend in; `XTREAM_TV_BUILD=true` marks the package as a TV app (leanback) and bumps its version number into the TV slot.

## Installing the regular app on a TV instead

If you install the normal mobile APK (`Extreme-InfiniTV.apk`, or the versioned APK from [Releases](https://github.com/infinitel8p/Extreme-InfiniTV/releases/latest)) on a TV, it detects the TV and still boots into the receiver screen by default. The difference: here you can leave. The idle screen shows an **Exit receiver** button, and the back key also exits, dropping you into a TV-native browse UI built for the remote: Home, Live TV (with a per-channel programme guide and catch-up), Movies, Series, Search, Downloads, and Settings.

<Shot src="Android-TV/tv" alt="The TV home screen with the left rail and content rails" caption="The full app on Android TV: left rail, hero and rails, all driven by the remote." />

Sign in with the remote's on-screen keyboard, or paste a playlist link on the login screen and it fills in the server/username/password (or M3U URL) for you. Pushing credentials over from your phone is planned but not built yet. A few things are deliberately off this surface to keep it usable from the couch - the playlist editor, channel overrides, category management, external players, and a handful of other desktop/mobile-only settings stay on the phone/desktop app.

The dedicated TV build from the Play Store stays receiver-only; it never gets this browse UI. To get it on a TV, sideload the mobile APK instead - the same "Installing on Fire TV" instructions below work on any Android TV device, not just Fire TV.

On this install, receiver mode itself and whether it starts on boot are both toggles in the **TV receiver** section of the **Casting** card, under **Settings > Network**:

- **Allow casting to this device** - turns receiving on or off.
- **Start on receiver screen** - launches straight to the pairing screen like a dedicated receiver; exit it anytime and it comes back next launch. This one is also in the TV browse UI's own Settings screen, alongside a shortcut back to the receiver screen.

<Callout type="note">
  These two toggles only appear on the mobile build. The dedicated TV build has no Settings screen to toggle them from - it's always on.
</Callout>

## Switching between the two builds

The receiver build carries a higher internal version number than the mobile build. Installing receiver over mobile works as a normal update, but going the other way - receiver back to mobile - needs an uninstall first, since Android won't let a lower version number install over a higher one.

## Pairing

Sender and TV need to be on the same network - this is a LAN protocol, so nothing leaves your home.

The TV's idle screen shows everything the sender asks you for: the device name, a pair code, and the LAN addresses it is listening on.

<Steps>

1. On the TV, open the receiver screen. The dedicated TV build boots straight into it; on the mobile build, turn on **Allow casting to this device** in Settings, or use **Start on receiver screen**.
2. On the sender, open the **Play on TV** picker - from a poster's context menu, or the **Play on TV** section of the Casting card under **Settings > Network**.
3. Pick the TV from the scan. If it doesn't appear, add it by one of the addresses shown on the TV.
4. Enter the pair code from the TV's screen.

</Steps>

Once paired, the sender remembers the TV for next time.

## Controlling what's playing

While a cast is running, the pill at the bottom of the sender opens the remote: play/pause, seek, volume, and what's on now and next. The remote also lets you switch what is playing without leaving it: for a live channel, **Channels** (or <kbd>C</kbd>) browses the playlist by group, following the same visibility and sort settings as the Live TV page; for a series, the same spot becomes **Episodes** and browses by season. The search box spans everything from either level.

## Installing on Fire TV

Fire TV isn't on the Play Store, so the way in is sideloading with the [AFTVnews Downloader](https://www.aftvnews.com/downloader/) app: install Downloader from the Fire TV app store, open it, and enter one of these codes in its URL field.

- **`8281651`** (short URL `aftv.news/8281651`) - the full app (`Extreme-InfiniTV.apk`), with the browse UI described above. This is what you want if you're setting the TV up as a normal client.
- **`1317634`** (short URL `aftv.news/1317634`) - the receiver-only kiosk build (`Extreme-InfiniTV-receiver.apk`). No browse UI, no exit; it just sits on the pairing screen waiting for a cast.

<Callout type="note">
  Picking the wrong one is the most common mix-up here: the receiver build can only be cast to, not browsed. If you want Home, Live TV, Movies, Series, and the rest on the TV itself, use `8281651`.
</Callout>

Both codes always point at the current stable release, so they keep working after updates. `adb install Extreme-InfiniTV.apk` (or `-receiver.apk`) works the same way on any Android TV device if you'd rather sideload without Downloader.
