## Sign in

The login screen has one **Server URL** field plus optional **Username** / **Password**. How you fill it in depends on what your provider gave you.

### Xtream Codes

If your provider sent you a host, a port, a username, and a password, fill in:

- **Server URL**: your provider's host, plus the port if they gave you one. For example `example-iptv.tv:8080` or `http://example-iptv.tv`
- **Username** and **Password**: exactly as your provider sent them.

<Callout type="tip">
  Many providers also send a long "player_api.php" URL with the username and password baked in. Paste that whole URL into **Server URL** and the app auto-fills the username and password fields for you - again, the `http://` prefix is optional.
</Callout>

### M3U / M3U8 URL

If your provider only gave you a single playlist URL (anything ending in `.m3u` or `.m3u8`, or a `get.php?...` link), paste the **whole URL** into **Server URL** and leave Username and Password blank. The app detects the M3U format and switches modes.

<Callout type="tip">
  URLs with credentials baked in, like `https://user:password@example.com/playlist.m3u`, work too. The app sends the credentials as HTTP Basic authentication when loading the playlist and its streams.
</Callout>

A bare direct stream link works too: paste a single `.m3u8` URL that isn't a playlist, just the stream itself, and the app adds it as a playlist with that one playable channel.

### Playlist file from your device

Got a `.m3u` file instead of a URL? Use **Choose file...** on the login screen to load it straight from your device. The playlist is stored locally, so it keeps working with no server involved.

<Callout type="note">
  Just want to try the app? The login screen also offers a **Demo (iptv-org)** playlist - a free public playlist you can remove at any time.
</Callout>

### EPG sources

The login screen's advanced options let you attach one or more **EPG URLs** to a playlist (merged in order, with a **Test sources** button), disable the provider's own EPG, and add **backup domains** (mirrors) for Xtream accounts - if the main server stops responding, the app fails over to the next mirror automatically.

For M3U playlists, the app also picks up EPG sources the playlist itself already points to: if the file lists more than one guide URL (`x-tvg-url`, `tvg-url`, or `url-tvg`, comma-separated), all of them get merged in automatically.

### Personalize a playlist

Still in the login screen's advanced options, you can give a playlist a **Name**, an **Emoji**, and an **Accent color**. The emoji replaces the type badge next to that playlist wherever it's listed, and the accent color overrides the app's accent while that playlist is active - handy for telling accounts apart at a glance.

## Multiple playlists

You can add as many Xtream accounts and M3U URLs as you want. Open **Settings > Playlists** to add, rename, or remove them. Switch the active one from the sidebar.

Favorites, watchlist, recents, and watch progress are tracked **per playlist** so different accounts stay separate. Favorites, Watchlist, and Continue watching also offer a combined view across all your playlists.

## Custom playlists

You can also build a channel list of your own: open **Settings > Playlists > New custom playlist** and pull channels in from any of your existing playlists, or grab a stream straight from a web page with [Add from website](./add-from-website/). See [Custom playlists](./custom-playlists/) for the full guide.

## Hide unwanted categories

Many providers ship hundreds of categories you don't want to see ("Adult", regional channels, dead links). Open **Settings > Library > Categories** (or the filter button on Live TV / Movies / Series) to toggle which ones show up. Hidden categories stay hidden across restarts and are remembered per playlist.

For M3U playlists, if a channel's `group-title` lists more than one group separated by semicolons, the app puts that channel in every group listed instead of just the first one.

## Missing channel logos

If a channel doesn't have a logo from your provider, the app fills it in automatically from [iptv-org](https://github.com/iptv-org)'s free, open logo collection. It's fetched once and cached on your device, then matched to your channels by `tvg-id` first, falling back to the channel name.

## Check on a playlist's health

**Settings > Playlists > Active playlist health** shows your account status (active / expired), the expiry date, how many connections you're using out of your provider's allowance, and when each catalog (live / movies / series / EPG) last loaded successfully. It's the first place to look when something stops loading.

## EPG looks shifted

If the EPG (now / next, full schedule) looks off by a few hours, your provider is sending timestamps in their own timezone rather than UTC. Open **Settings > Watching > EPG time zone** and adjust **Offset for active playlist**. The full schedule is on the **EPG** page in the sidebar.

For M3U playlists, if just one or two channels look shifted while the rest are fine, the playlist itself may set a `tvg-shift` on those channels - the app honors it for the guide display automatically, no setting needed.

## Backup and restore

**Settings > Data > Backup** exports a single JSON file containing your playlists, preferences, and app settings. Import it on another device to bring everything across.

<Callout type="warning">
  The export includes your credentials in plain JSON. Treat the file like a password manager export: don't email it, don't drop it in a shared folder, and delete it from any temporary location once you've restored on the target device.
</Callout>

## Export a playlist

Every playlist row in **Settings > Playlists** has an **Export M3U** action that writes a standard `.m3u` file you can use in other apps.
